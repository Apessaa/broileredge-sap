# Expert evaluation protocol (ex ante, artificial: EVAL2 and part of EVAL3), for paper v34

**What it adds to the paper:** the paper shows that the thresholds and tasks trace to published sources
(O3) and that the whole flow works in emulation. It does not yet show whether practitioners judge the
rules, the possible causes and the tasks appropriate (Section VI, construct threat; reviewer concern #1).
This protocol asks real experts to rate them after a short briefing, on one anonymous form where every
answer is a rating from 1 to 5. In FEDS terms it is a formative, artificial evaluation episode.
**The results must come from real people. Do not fill in, simulate or "complete" any answer, and do not
let a tool fill in a form on an expert's behalf.**

## 1. Panel (8–9 people)

| Group | Number | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Closed-house practice | at least 5 | farm heads, house managers, technical service staff, poultry veterinarians |
| SAP | 2 | PM or MM consultants, SAP key users |
| Information systems | 1–2 | IS lecturers or researchers outside the author team |

- **Why at least 5 practitioners:** the rule items and Part B are mainly for them (the others are asked to choose TD where an item is outside their field). With 3 raters an
  item passes only if all agree, and even 3 of 3 gives a 95% lower bound of 0.29.
- **Inclusion:** at least 2 years in the role, checked when inviting, not on the form. The authors and their supervisors do not take part.
- **Recruitment:** use your own professional networks. Ask the person, not their employer, and collect no company data.

## 2. Session (about 35 minutes, online or in person, one expert at a time)

1. **Consent (2 min):** the expert reads page 1 and ticks the consent box (check that it is ticked before the
   briefing starts). Nothing about the expert is asked or written.
2. **Briefing (8 min):** the Indonesian briefing slides, read from the script, the same for every expert,
   including the limits of the design (emulated sensors, mock SAP, untuned limits).
3. **Parts A and B (20 min):** the expert fills in the form alone. Do not watch the form while it is
   being filled in; for paper forms, collect it in an envelope. Say once: "We are looking for weaknesses;
   low ratings are as useful as high ones."
4. **Close (2 min):** thank the expert.

Answer factual questions about the design, but do not argue for it.

## 3. Data entry

Give each form a running row number (`R01`, `R02`, …) only when you type it in; the number is not
written on the form and is linked to no person. Type each form into one row of
`responses_template.csv` (save it as `responses.csv`):
- **Part A:** items 1–20 in form order: `R1`–`R7` (items 1–7), `T1`–`T5` (8–12), `CHK` (13), `S1`–`S5`
  (14–18), `G1`, `G2` (19–20): `1`–`5`, or `TD`.
- **Part B:** `C1_level` … `C9_level`; for cases 2–7 and 9 also `_cause` and `_tasks`: `1`–`5`.

Leave a cell empty if the expert left it empty, and enter only what the expert marked. `import_forms.py` also
reports whether the consent box on page 1 is ticked; for a form without a tick, record how that expert consented
(e.g. orally before the briefing) before the answers are used, and state it in the paper. Keep the paper
forms (or files) as evidence, filed only under their row number. For online sessions, ask the expert to
return the form in one message, save it as `R01.pdf` (or `.docx`), and delete the message and its
attachment once saved, so no file links an answer to a sender.

## 4. Analysis (rules fixed before data collection)

```
python3 analyse_expert.py responses.csv
```

This writes `results_expert.md` and `results_expert.json` next to `responses.csv`.

- **Who counts for an item:** every expert who gave a rating 1–5. TD answers (an item outside the expert's
  field) are excluded and counted.
- **Per item and per Part B rating:** n, median, IQR, I-CVI (share of ratings 4–5) with an exact
  (Clopper–Pearson) 95% CI, modified kappa κ* (Polit, Beck & Owen 2007), Aiken's V (Aiken 1985), and
  a_wg (Brown & Hauenstein 2005, for n ≥ 4). S-CVI/Ave for the seven rule items and for each Part B rating.
- **Decision per item:** fewer than 3 raters → too few raters; κ* < 0.60 or median ≤ 3 → review the rule or
  task; I-CVI meeting Lynn's rule (no disagreement for n ≤ 5, at most one for n ≥ 6), median ≥ 4 and
  IQR ≤ 1 → accepted; otherwise → revise.
- **Risk statements T5 (alarm fatigue) and S5 (administrative burden):** reported as they are; ratings 4–5
  mean the expert sees the risk. They are findings, not instrument failures, and are never reversed and
  averaged with other items.
- **Attention check (item 13):** the correct answer is 1–2 (one notification needs three Severe episodes
  in 24 hours). It is reported, and the analysis is repeated without experts who answered 4–5.
- **Sensitivity:** every result is also given for the experts who passed the check only.
- The form records no role, so results are not split by group; the paper reports the panel from the tally.
